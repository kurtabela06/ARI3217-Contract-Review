# RD1 AI Act classification

The intended operational use is to highlight clauses in commercial contracts
for a private legal team before signing. The API returns cited passages, and
the lawyer checks them against the original agreement and decides how to proceed.

For that intended use, I assess the system as outside the high-risk categories
in Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. It is a standalone review aid rather
than a product safety component covered by Article 6(1). Under Article 6(2),
Annex III, point 8(a), concerns AI that supports judicial authorities in
interpreting facts and law or applying law to a case, including similar uses in
alternative dispute resolution. The private pre-signing workflow does not
perform that function. Human oversight reduces review risk. The classification
follows from the intended use.

The current RD1 prototype is limited to local development and benchmark
testing. Article 2(8) excludes development and testing before placement on the
market or putting into service, except for testing in real-world conditions.

Under Article 3(3) and (4), the provider would be the entity that develops or
commissions the system and supplies it or puts it into service under its own
name. The deployer would be the firm or organisation using it under its
authority. A firm that builds and puts the tool into service for its own legal
team could be both. The reviewing lawyer is the user and owns the review
decision.

Reassess the classification before repurposing the tool to help a court or
arbitral tribunal interpret evidence and apply law to a dispute. That purpose
could bring it within Annex III, point 8(a), even if a human still decides.
The assessment would then need to consider the specific conditions in
Article 6(3).

[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 2, 3 and 6 and Annex III](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689)
