# RD1 personal reflection

The passage-splitting result made me think about how easily a clause can lose its meaning. For 6.47% of annotated clauses, no single passage contained at least 90% of the clause. A highlight could show the main obligation while leaving its exception in the next passage. I would check the surrounding text before relying on a highlight. [Data findings](data-findings.md)

The dashboard showed me that the API was healthy and returning results. Seeing about 88% red-flag recall on regular contracts could have made me confident in the tool overall, but recall was about 53% on held-out franchise agreements. I would report the two results separately and route franchise agreements to full manual review. I would use lawyer-verified results and actual franchise review volumes to decide whether retraining was worth the cost. [Recall evidence](evidence-index.md#model-data-and-architecture)

I would start with the online API and keep the lawyer in the loop. The online phase took 63.74 s, and the batch published all packs in 63.69 s. The batch workflow makes the packs available together at the end. If peak load pushed p95 above the measured online reference of 130.81 ms and delayed same-session review, I would trial tiered access. The entry tier would get a quota of immediate reviews and queue extra contracts for quieter periods. The higher tier would get priority. I would switch only if priority p95 recovered and queued packs arrived by an agreed deadline. See the [matched benchmark](evidence-index.md#matched-online-and-batch-test).
